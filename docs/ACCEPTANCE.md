## Draft algorithms — 19 October 2026
- The Sports.ru club rule (at most two players of one NBA club) is a hard draft rule: `pick()` refuses a third, and the exact completion solver is now a min-cost circulation source → club (≤2) → player → position (=2) with lower bounds for fixed picks; "at least one rookie" is met by forcing each rookie in turn when the unconstrained optimum has none. A club-aware greedy pass that reaches the unconstrained lower bound is accepted as optimal; interchangeable players are pruned by an exchange argument. 3,000 random brute-force cases (single and multi-position, clubs, fixed picks, budgets) agree exactly; 400 of them run in the test suite. The plan MILP and the rookie timing study apply the same rule.
- Survival to our next picks counts only opponents' picks: our own simulated pick censors the player (Kaplan–Meier over the gaps between our picks); a player our bot always takes borrows the hazard of the ten players ranked next to him. Before, our own bot's choice counted as a loss (Durant to pick 25: 0.18 instead of 0.44).
- Bots keep one style per draft. Their rookie habit is drawn from the rounds in which the FBL GMs took rookies in the real 2026 draft (picks 32–79); a team takes its rookie earlier when the rookies it could still use are no more than the teams still needing one. Simulated rookies now go at picks 25–76 instead of always 41–48.
- Rollout ties use the Student t critical value for the number of paired runs (4.30 for three), not 2. [Not in the UI since #81: only `analyzeRecommendations` uses it.]
- The device stores a draft on the automatic database as its picks and the picked players' snapshots (kilobytes instead of 3.7 MB); the catalog comes from the cached or fetched database. Saves without a price mode keep the cap on, as imports do. The cap and roster sizes come from `data/league-rules.json`.
- Imported links render only for http(s) URLs; imported forecasts must be numeric. In training mode Undo returns to before my last pick; in live mode a rival's real pick that the app thinks leaves no completion can still be recorded after confirmation.

## Pipeline reliability — 19 October 2026
- The season watcher never starts a full Sports.ru collection in its last 15 minutes and caps it at the time left, so a slow collection cannot run into the job timeout (which would skip the step that starts the next run and break the chain).

## Availability as a Markov chain of absences — 19 October 2026
- Every absence in the 2002–2026 regular-season logs is rebuilt in club games: the spell before a player's first game, between games and after his last (an incomplete club calendar in the older career logs is counted from days at the season's game spacing; on the complete seasons the rebuilt season length is 81.9 games). The logs cannot tell injury from rest or a coach's decision; for fantasy they cost the same.
- The season is a semi-Markov chain over the team games: from an available game a short (1–3 games) or long (4+) spell starts with the player's hazard, a Gamma-Poisson posterior of his own spells over recent available games (older seasons × 0.6) shrunk to his age group; spell lengths follow the league's Kaplan–Meier distribution (spells running at season end are censored); the season starts out with the frequency seen after a similar previous season (healthy 26%, ending it out 10+ games 38%, a season without games 74%). A Gamma season multiplier (shape 2.5) makes some seasons worse than others. The games distribution is solved exactly by dynamic programming; a 20,000-run simulation agrees in the tests.
- Its own mean is not used: forward-tested 2020–2026 it lost to the least-squares share regression (games MAE 18.4 vs 15.4), and averaging the two lost too. The hazards are scaled so the chain's mean equals the forecast games (regression, or its mean with ESPN), and the chain supplies the shape. Parameters were chosen forward on 2021–2026 by the log score of the actual games; that range includes the held-out 2025/26, and a choice on 2021–2025 alone keeps the same parameters (audit 21.10).
- Held out 2025/26: 80% games range covers 79.6% (no games range existed before); the season range now pairs the neighbours' points-per-game errors with the chain's games: 73.8% inside the 80% range (73.2% before), below the range 11.1% (11.8%), above 15.1% (15.1%). Short-season Brier 0.133 (0.133 before, base rate 0.159). [Without the result bonus (20.10): games range 79.8%; season range 74.6% inside, 10.3% below, 15.1% above; Brier 0.133 vs 0.158. All of these use the model's games, not ESPN's.] What is left above the range is per-game breakouts: 2025/26 had a p90 rate error of +37% against +19–26% in 2020–2025, which no calibration on earlier seasons can foresee.
- Player cards and transfer explanations show the player's short and long spells per 82 games against his age group, how he ended last season and his 80% games range.
- A probability of an absence is not a scheduled absence. Two places now start the chain from what is known today:
  - Pre-season: a player with no injury on the current ESPN report starts the season healthy. His model games move from the unconditional mean (which includes the league's chance of starting the season out) towards the chain's mean started healthy, by a weight per last-season ending fitted on 2021–2025 (healthy 1.0, ended out 10+ games 0.3, no games 0.2): players who ended a season injured still play fewer games when healthy at the start (208 cases: 56.4 played, 52.8 unconditional, 62.1 healthy-start chain). Held out 2025/26, 296 players who played their club's first game: games MAE 13.4 vs 14.1, bias −0.7 vs −4.2. The proxy (played the first game) is a little stronger than a clean preseason report.
  - In season: each coming club game counts with the chance to be available that many healthy games ahead (from today, or from an injured player's return date), the chain scaled to the player's updated season share. Backtest 2025/26, players who played their club's last game: next 3 games played 86.1% (season share 78.2%, chain 89.3%), next 7 games 83.7% (78.2%, 86.4%; MAE 0.194 → 0.165), next 28 games 78.1% (78.2%, 81.6%). The flat share under-valued healthy players over a week or a month; over the rest of a season the two agree.
- Data limits: no historical injury reports (body part, expected return) are in the collection. ESPN's news feed keeps about nine months per player (for example Zubac's 2025/26: a sprained left ankle, 15 games, then a fractured rib to the end of the season), Rotowire's reports are paid. An injury-type multiplier (knee, Achilles) could not be validated on outcomes, so it is not applied; current injury knowledge enters through the ESPN report (healthy start, return dates) and ESPN's projected games, half of the combined mean.
