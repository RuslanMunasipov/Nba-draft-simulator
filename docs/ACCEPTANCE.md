## Rookie round opportunity-cost study
- A Web Worker compares each remaining first-rookie target round using 12 paired seeds (24 before the club rule made each draft dearer) in each of four explicit GM demand profiles (rounds 2–4, 4–6, 6–8 and mixed 1–10): 480 complete drafts from a fresh state.
- All scored players must have comparable individual ESPN component-season forecasts. Unknown forecasts are excluded, never zero-filled; a picked player missing a comparable forecast prevents the study.
- Straight order, positions, uniqueness, rookie completion and optional verified salary cap are checked. Invalid runs are reported and disqualify that strategy, rather than improving its mean by being silently dropped.
- Main objective: maximum mean full-roster component FP at equal ASSUMED profile weights among scenarios compatible with recorded GM picks. Contradicted profiles are explicitly excluded; this is not a calibrated Bayesian probability. A separate minimax-regret choice and scenario-specific optima expose sensitivity; no scenario frequency is claimed to be a calibrated GM pick probability.
- Opponent veteran order uses ADP; rookie order uses projected components with randomized top-three choices and profile-specific timing. The user's veteran picks are greedy forecast choices with positional/rookie reservation, not a proof of a globally optimal draft policy.
- PF and minute/result bonuses remain excluded. A small score gap cannot establish a real advantage. Actual FBL GM preferences and increased mandatory-rookie demand need live observations; the study updates from recorded picks automatically.

## Career projection model — 19 October 2026
- The collected career game logs (ESPN, NBA regular seasons 2002–2026, 216k games of the current catalog) are kept permanently in `data/raw/career_games.parquet`; the Actions artifact expires on 16 January 2027. The Sports.ru formula on these logs matches official Sports.ru season totals 2018/19–2025/26 with a median gap of 1–2 FP per season.
- `scripts/projection_model.py` forecasts points per minute, minutes per game and the share of team games separately (Marcel-style weights of the last three seasons and the career, shrinkage, age curves), every weight fitted on target seasons 2014–2024/25. Levels and age curves come from the last three seasons only: older transitions are survivors (players still in the league in 2026), which otherwise biased forecasts upward by about one FP per game.
- Held out, untouched 2025/26 season (376 players of current rosters): season MAE 262 vs 310 for repeating the previous season (paired 95% gain 33–62 FP); FP/game 2.38 vs 2.63; games 15.8 vs 19.1. The build fails if the lower end of that gain falls to zero.
- Availability is a least-squares regression on the last seasons, the career share, career length and age (fitted on the last six seasons): forward-tested 2020–2026 it beats the earlier shrinkage (15.0 vs 15.2 games MAE) and no longer under-predicts the bounce-back after a season cut short by injury (predicted 0.64 of games, played 0.67; before 0.54).
- Season range: out-of-sample errors of earlier seasons, each predicted by a model fitted only on the seasons before it, chosen by predicted availability, record length and age, then scaled per tail by leave-one-season-out conformal calibration. On 2025/26: 72% inside the 80% range (11% below, 17% above; young players with short records break out more often than past seasons showed). The weak season is a real floor (median 39% of the expectation) instead of the previous ~0%. Short-season risk (under a quarter of games) beats the base rate (Brier 0.094 vs 0.097).
- Rookies: rate, minutes and share by draft pick, fitted on the complete 2024 draft class and checked on the 2025 class (113 players): FP/game error 2.96 vs 3.29 for one flat prior, share 0.199 vs 0.283. Rookie ranges use that class's out-of-sample errors. Draft classes 2003–2026 are in `data/nba-drafts.json`.
- Where ESPN has an individual projection the forecast is the equal-weight mean of ESPN and the model; there is no archive of past ESPN projections to fit a weight. The 17 October ESPN snapshot is frozen so the 2026/27 season can measure it.
- Team result bonus: the team's previous season shrunk by its measured persistence (slope 0.62 over 452 team-seasons). Season errors of teammates are uncorrelated (−0.8% over 12,927 pairs), so place scenarios draw players independently; a league-wide swing does not change places and is not simulated.
- One metric everywhere: `projectedFP` is the full-formula per-game forecast. The fixed value penalties are removed; spread labels compare a player with the other priced players.
- History is joined to ESPN rosters by reviewed ids before names (no duplicate Yang Hansen).

## Draft algorithms — 19 October 2026
- The Sports.ru club rule (at most two players of one NBA club) is a hard draft rule: `pick()` refuses a third, and the exact completion solver is now a min-cost circulation source → club (≤2) → player → position (=2) with lower bounds for fixed picks; "at least one rookie" is met by forcing each rookie in turn when the unconstrained optimum has none. A club-aware greedy pass that reaches the unconstrained lower bound is accepted as optimal; interchangeable players are pruned by an exchange argument. 3,000 random brute-force cases (single and multi-position, clubs, fixed picks, budgets) agree exactly; 400 of them run in the test suite. The plan MILP and the rookie timing study apply the same rule.
- Survival to our next picks counts only opponents' picks: our own simulated pick censors the player (Kaplan–Meier over the gaps between our picks); a player our bot always takes borrows the hazard of the ten players ranked next to him. Before, our own bot's choice counted as a loss (Durant to pick 25: 0.18 instead of 0.44).
- Bots keep one style per draft. Their rookie habit is drawn from the rounds in which the FBL GMs took rookies in the real 2026 draft (picks 32–79); a team takes its rookie earlier when the rookies it could still use are no more than the teams still needing one. Simulated rookies now go at picks 25–76 instead of always 41–48.
- Rollout ties use the Student t critical value for the number of paired runs (4.30 for three), not 2.
- The device stores a draft on the automatic database as its picks and the picked players' snapshots (kilobytes instead of 3.7 MB); the catalog comes from the cached or fetched database. Saves without a price mode keep the cap on, as imports do. The cap and roster sizes come from `data/league-rules.json`.
- Imported links render only for http(s) URLs; imported forecasts must be numeric. In training mode Undo returns to before my last pick; in live mode a rival's real pick that the app thinks leaves no completion can still be recorded after confirmation.

## Availability as a Markov chain of absences — 19 October 2026
- Every absence in the 2002–2026 regular-season logs is rebuilt in club games: the spell before a player's first game, between games and after his last (an incomplete club calendar in the older career logs is counted from days at the season's game spacing; on the complete seasons the rebuilt season length is 81.9 games). The logs cannot tell injury from rest or a coach's decision; for fantasy they cost the same.
- The season is a semi-Markov chain over the team games: from an available game a short (1–3 games) or long (4+) spell starts with the player's hazard, a Gamma-Poisson posterior of his own spells over recent available games (older seasons × 0.6) shrunk to his age group; spell lengths follow the league's Kaplan–Meier distribution (spells running at season end are censored); the season starts out with the frequency seen after a similar previous season (healthy 26%, ending it out 10+ games 38%, a season without games 74%). A Gamma season multiplier (shape 2.5) makes some seasons worse than others. The games distribution is solved exactly by dynamic programming; a 20,000-run simulation agrees in the tests.
- Its own mean is not used: forward-tested 2020–2026 it lost to the least-squares share regression (games MAE 18.4 vs 15.4), and averaging the two lost too. The hazards are scaled so the chain's mean equals the forecast games (regression, or its mean with ESPN), and the chain supplies the shape. Parameters were chosen forward on 2021–2026 by the log score of the actual games.
- Held out 2025/26: 80% games range covers 79.6% (no games range existed before); the season range now pairs the neighbours' points-per-game errors with the chain's games: 73.8% inside the 80% range (73.2% before), below the range 11.1% (11.8%), above 15.1% (15.1%). Short-season Brier 0.133 (0.133 before, base rate 0.159). What is left above the range is per-game breakouts: 2025/26 had a p90 rate error of +37% against +19–26% in 2020–2025, which no calibration on earlier seasons can foresee.
- Player cards and transfer explanations show the player's short and long spells per 82 games against his age group, how he ended last season and his 80% games range.
- A probability of an absence is not a scheduled absence. Two places now start the chain from what is known today:
  - Pre-season: a player with no injury on the current ESPN report starts the season healthy. His model games move from the unconditional mean (which includes the league's chance of starting the season out) towards the chain's mean started healthy, by a weight per last-season ending fitted on 2021–2025 (healthy 1.0, ended out 10+ games 0.3, no games 0.2): players who ended a season injured still play fewer games when healthy at the start (208 cases: 56.4 played, 52.8 unconditional, 62.1 healthy-start chain). Held out 2025/26, 296 players who played their club's first game: games MAE 13.4 vs 14.1, bias −0.7 vs −4.2. The proxy (played the first game) is a little stronger than a clean preseason report.
  - In season: each coming club game counts with the chance to be available that many healthy games ahead (from today, or from an injured player's return date), the chain scaled to the player's updated season share. Backtest 2025/26, players who played their club's last game: next 3 games played 86.1% (season share 78.2%, chain 89.3%), next 7 games 83.7% (78.2%, 86.4%; MAE 0.194 → 0.165), next 28 games 78.1% (78.2%, 81.6%). The flat share under-valued healthy players over a week or a month; over the rest of a season the two agree.
- Data limits: no historical injury reports (body part, expected return) are in the collection. ESPN's news feed keeps about nine months per player (for example Zubac's 2025/26: a sprained left ankle, 15 games, then a fractured rib to the end of the season), Rotowire's reports are paid. An injury-type multiplier (knee, Achilles) could not be validated on outcomes, so it is not applied; current injury knowledge enters through the ESPN report (healthy start, return dates) and ESPN's projected games, half of the combined mean.

## Roster slot value with weekly transfers, injury archive — 20 October 2026
- A long absence costs a fantasy team only until the player is replaced (three Sports.ru transfers a week). The draft now ranks a roster slot: the player's own season plus a replacement's points in the games of his absences of 4+ games after a two-game reaction delay (he is bought back on his return). Expected replaced games come from the same absence chain; the replacement is the best player left at the same Sports.ru position after the league's 16 starters there (ranks 17–24 by forecast per game) at a healthy player's chance to play. Team scenarios add the same replacement points.
- Held out 2025/26 (485 players; the replacement level is the same on both sides, so only the absence model is tested): ranking of what each slot actually produced, own points plus a replacement over the actual long absences — Spearman 0.64 with replacements vs 0.55 own forecast; slot MAE 186 vs 323; the 40 best slots by the new value produced 1.8% more (top 80: 0.6%). Expected replaced games 20.3, actual 23.3.
- Assumptions, not fitted: the two-game delay (at a mean of 60 games the replaced games are about 13.5, 12.3, 11 and 9 at delays 1, 2, 3 and 5), buying the player back, and price changes of injured players, which are ignored.
- The season watcher now archives every ESPN injury report into `injury-history.json` on `data/season-live`: one episode per player and injury (body part, kind such as fracture or sprain, side, first and last report, return date). ESPN keeps only the current report and about nine months of news, so this archive is what will let a risk per body part be fitted on outcomes after a season.
- Transfer cards summarise the injuries named in a player's ESPN news (body part, first and last mention) and, once collected, the archived report episodes. Both are descriptive, not forecast inputs.

- The «белое правило» binds the advice like the rookie rule: a swap may sell the roster's last counting player only for another one on the league list, packages keep at least one, and the draft plan keeps one in the ten (main.js reads the list from `data/fbl-league.json`, rebuilt with every league-log commit). Without a list nothing is constrained.
- League protocols never reach the personal chat: the one exception is the manual `League feed sample` workflow (one protocol headed as a sample, sent only when the owner dispatches it, to forward to the commissioner); the feed refuses the personal bot or chat even in its test mode (`LEAGUE_FEED_PREVIEW`). The personal bot keeps only the personal alerts and advice.
- Every Telegram message (personal alerts, league feed, watcher service alerts) ends with the Moscow time it was sent. Sports.ru journals give only the date of a transfer, so the watcher stamps each journal row with the time it first saw it (`seenAt`, within a minute of the transfer); transfer alerts and protocols show that Moscow time. Transfers made before the watch began show their date only.
- Every league-feed protocol carries a photo of Зак Онн for its verdict (`scripts/assets/zak-onn/`): an obvious breach (no rookie, the NBA club or FBL transfer limit, a team the league found without a white player) opens with «🚨 НАРУШЕНИЕ РЕГЛАМЕНТА» and the thumbs-down photo; a doubt (the only marked white player sold with no other marked, or a roster the league has not decided on) opens with «🔎 ТРЕБУЕТСЯ ПРОВЕРКА ЮРИСТА» and the frowning photo; a clean roster gets the thumbs-up photo and «✅ Одобрено: Зак Онн». Breaches and doubts tag Зак Онн's operator and the commissioner by Telegram username, so Telegram notifies them personally. The protocol is the photo's caption (every protocol so far is under Telegram's 1024-character caption limit); a longer one follows the photo as its own message.
- FBL transfer hours (league owner, 20.10): transfers only from 08:00 to 22:00 Moscow time (`fblTransfers.hours` in `data/league-rules.json`). Sports.ru journals give only the date, so the watcher bounds the time of each new row: `seenAfter` is its last read of that journal without the row (kept every minute as `journalReadAt`, published with the next change), `seenAt` its first read with it. Inside the hours throughout: ✅; outside throughout: an obvious breach (thumbs-down photo, Зак Онн and the commissioner tagged); a window crossing 08:00 or 22:00 (including a watcher gap overnight): «требуется проверка» with the window shown. A row without `seenAfter` counts as made in the two minutes before `seenAt`; transfers seen before the watch began show «не зафиксировано» and are not judged. Each protocol judges the hours of its own deals only.
- The league chat starts clean (league owner, 20.10): `leagueFeed.since` in `data/league-rules.json` — transfers the watcher saw before it, or never timed, get no protocol; numbering starts at № 1 from there. A preview still shows every transfer since the draft.
- Each team's GM is tagged on a breach or a check of its roster.
- Telegram usernames are kept in the repository only encrypted (`data/telegram-handles.enc.json`, AES-256-GCM via `scripts/handles.mjs`); the key is derived from the league bot's token (`LEAGUE_TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` secret; `LEAGUE_HANDLES_KEY` overrides it), so a new bot token means encrypting the file again. They are decrypted in memory only when the league feed or an announcement is sent, never reach the site bundle, and without the key people are named, not tagged. Announcements write tags as `{@lawyer}`, `{@commissioner}`, `{@gm:<team>}`; one that cannot resolve its tags is refused.
- Statements of Зак Онн (`data/announcements/*.html`, Telegram HTML) go to the league chat only through the manual `League announcement` workflow, with one of his photos (`check`, `approved`, `violation`); the personal bot or chat is refused.
- Зак Онн's voice (`scripts/zak-voice.mjs`): the facts of a protocol stay as the rules check found them; he adds a heading («⚖️ ЗАК ОНН НЕДОВОЛЕН» on a breach, «🔎 ЗАК ОНН ПРОВЕРЯЕТ» on a doubt), one remark chosen by the verdict, the first broken rule and his watchlist (`watchlist` in `data/league-rules.json`: School Shooters Team since 20.10, shown as «🎯 Под прицелом Зака Онна»), and a rotating signature. The choice depends only on the protocol, so a protocol always reads the same. Conclusions name the broken rules briefly so a protocol fits under his photo (Telegram caption limit 1024).
- Зак Онн has 135 lines: by verdict, by broken or doubtful rule (time at the 22:00 edge, a sold white player, an undecided roster), several rules at once, the watchlist, the deal's circumstances (the week's last transfer, an empty or full bank, a buy of 2200+, a package, the week's first transfer) and dumb American lawyer jokes in every other protocol. The circumstance and joke lines are dropped again when the protocol would not fit under his photo.
- Pre-season transfers are free (league owner, 20.10): while Sports.ru shows ∞ transfers, nothing counts against the FBL week. The collector reads «∞» (`transfersUnlimited`), the watcher marks rows it sees then as `free`, and transfers dated before `fblTransfers.countFrom` (21.10) are free too; protocols say «предсезонье, без лимита».
- A team that trades back to exactly its draft roster keeps both transfers (the draft point is the first time the journal's roster equals the draft, and rows of one date are put picks-first: Sports.ru lists them in no stable order). Journal rows are matched by content when stamping, so a reordered journal never re-stamps old rows, and a reorder alone is not a change.
- Protocol numbers count posted messages (`count` in `league-state.json`): a day's package posted again after a new deal gets the next number.
